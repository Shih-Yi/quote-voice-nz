# Direct Response Copy - Conversion Copywriting Engine

Generate copy that converts using proven direct response principles. This skill takes a positioning angle (ideally from the Positioning Angles skill) and produces deployment-ready copy assets across formats.

This is not brand copywriting. This is direct response — every word earns its place by moving the reader closer to action.

---

## Core Influences

The copy frameworks below are drawn from the masters. Use their principles, not their era's language:

- **Eugene Schwartz** — Stages of awareness, sophistication levels, channelling existing desire
- **Gary Halbert** — "A starving crowd" market selection, story-driven selling, urgency mechanics
- **Claude Hopkins** — Scientific advertising, testing, specificity, reason-why copy
- **David Ogilvy** — Research-first, long-form when justified, headline supremacy
- **Joe Sugarman** — "Slippery slide" readability, psychological triggers, the sole purpose of every sentence is to get the next sentence read
- **Gary Bencivenga** — Believability as the #1 conversion lever

## Phase 1: Audience Awareness Diagnosis

Before writing a single word, diagnose where the reader sits on Schwartz's 5 Levels of Awareness:

| Level | State | Copy Strategy |
|-------|-------|---------------|
| **Unaware** | Doesn't know they have a problem | Lead with story, pattern interrupt, or provocative question |
| **Problem Aware** | Feels the pain, doesn't know solutions exist | Agitate the problem, name their frustration, then reveal the category |
| **Solution Aware** | Knows solutions exist, hasn't found yours | Differentiate on mechanism, speed, proof, or niche fit |
| **Product Aware** | Knows your product, hasn't bought | Overcome objections, stack proof, reduce risk |
| **Most Aware** | Ready to buy, needs a nudge | Lead with offer, urgency, or exclusive deal |

**Critical rule:** The copy's opening must match the reader's awareness level. A headline for the "Unaware" looks completely different from one for the "Product Aware."

Ask the user to confirm or estimate the audience's awareness level. Default to **Problem Aware** if unknown — this is the most common starting point.

---

## Phase 2: Market Sophistication Check

Schwartz's 5 stages of market sophistication determine HOW you make your claim:

| Stage | Market State | Headline Strategy |
|-------|-------------|-------------------|
| **1** | First to market | Simple, direct claim: "Lose weight" |
| **2** | Competitors exist | Bigger claim: "Lose 10kg in 30 days" |
| **3** | Claims exhausted | Unique mechanism: "The Keto Enzyme Method" |
| **4** | Mechanisms exhausted | Expanded mechanism + proof: "The Enhanced Keto Enzyme — now with 3x absorption" |
| **5** | Total scepticism | Identity + story: "For the woman who's tried everything" |

Most markets today sit at Stage 3-5. NZ trade services market is Stage 2-3 for software tools — claims are being made but mechanisms are still differentiating.

## Phase 3: The Copy Brief

Assemble these elements before writing. Pull from the positioning-angles output if available:

1. **Primary positioning angle** — The single idea this copy will sell
2. **Target reader** — Specific person (job, pain, desire, language they use)
3. **Awareness level** — Where they are on the Schwartz scale
4. **Primary desire** — What they want MORE of (money, time, status, ease)
5. **Primary fear** — What they want LESS of (risk, effort, embarrassment, loss)
6. **Proof assets** — Testimonials, data, case studies, credentials available
7. **Offer details** — What they get, price, guarantee, bonuses
8. **CTA** — The single action we want them to take
9. **Tone** — Match the audience (e.g. tradies: direct, no-BS, mate-to-mate)

## Phase 4: Copy Generation — By Asset Type

### 4A: Landing Page Copy

Structure using the proven direct response landing page framework:

**1. Hero Section**
- **Headline:** The single biggest promise or pattern interrupt. Test 3 variations:
  - Power headline (benefit-first): "Send Professional Quotes in 60 Seconds — From Your Ute"
  - Problem headline (agitate-first): "Still Doing Quotes at the Kitchen Table at 9pm?"
  - Proof headline (credibility-first): "1,200+ NZ Tradies Stopped Losing Jobs to Slow Quotes"
- **Subheadline:** Expand the headline with specificity or mechanism
- **CTA button:** Action-oriented, first-person: "Start Quoting Faster" (not "Submit" or "Sign Up")
- **Social proof micro-element:** One line. "Trusted by 1,200+ Kiwi tradies" or a single star rating

**2. Problem Section (The Agitation)**
- Name the 3 most painful symptoms the reader experiences
- Use their language, not marketing language
- Format: Short paragraphs or bullet points. Each one a gut punch
- End with: "There's a better way" transition

**3. Solution Section (The Mechanism)**
- Introduce the product through its unique mechanism
- Explain HOW it works in 3 simple steps (the "1-2-3" framework)
- Each step: Icon + Short headline + One sentence
- Keep it stupid simple — if a 12-year-old can't follow it, simplify

**4. Benefits Section**
- 3-5 benefits, NOT features
- Format: "Benefit headline" + 1-2 sentence expansion
- Translate features into outcomes: "Voice recording" becomes "Quote from your ute without typing a word"
- Use the "So that..." test: every benefit must answer "so that the customer can..."

**5. Social Proof Section**
- Testimonials with: Name, trade, location, specific result
- Format: Pull-quote style with the most impactful sentence highlighted
- If no testimonials yet: Use founder story, beta user feedback, or "built by a tradie for tradies" credibility

**6. Objection Handling**
- FAQ-style or direct statements
- Address the top 3-4 objections:
  - "Is this hard to use?"
  - "What if I'm not tech savvy?"
  - "What does it cost?"
  - "Can I try it first?"
- Pre-empt with confidence, don't be defensive

**7. Final CTA Section**
- Restate the primary benefit
- Add urgency or scarcity if authentic (never fake)
- Repeat the CTA button
- Risk reversal: guarantee, free trial, "cancel anytime"

### 4B: Ad Copy (Facebook / Instagram / Google)

**Short-form ad (Primary text):**
- Hook (first line — must stop the scroll): Question, bold claim, or "If you... then..."
- Agitate: 1-2 sentences naming the pain
- Solution: 1 sentence introducing the product
- Proof: 1 social proof element
- CTA: Clear next step

**Variations per ad type:**

| Type | Format | Length |
|------|--------|--------|
| **Problem-agitate** | Lead with pain | 3-5 lines |
| **Testimonial-led** | Quote from user + context | 3-4 lines |
| **Listicle** | "3 reasons tradies are switching to..." | 4-6 lines |
| **Before/After** | Contrast old way vs new way | 4-5 lines |
| **Direct offer** | Lead with the deal/trial | 2-3 lines |

**Headlines (for ad creative):**
- Generate 5 variations, each under 40 characters
- Mix: benefit, curiosity, proof, urgency, identity

---

### 4C: Email Copy

**Welcome sequence (3 emails):**

**Email 1: The Promise (Day 0)**
- Subject line: Curiosity or benefit-driven (test 2 variations)
- Body: Welcome, restate the core value prop, set expectations, single CTA
- Tone: Warm, direct, no fluff

**Email 2: The Problem (Day 2)**
- Subject line: Agitate the core pain
- Body: Story or scenario that makes the reader feel their problem deeply
- Transition: "That's why we built [product]"
- CTA: Try the product / watch a demo

**Email 3: The Proof (Day 4)**
- Subject line: Social proof or result-driven
- Body: Case study, testimonial, or specific result
- CTA: Start using the product with urgency element

**Email principles:**
- Subject lines: 4-8 words, no clickbait, test curiosity vs. benefit
- Opening line: Never "Hi [Name], I hope this email finds you well"
- One CTA per email — never more
- PS line: Second hook or reminder of urgency

---

### 4D: Microcopy & UX Copy

- **Button text:** First-person, action-oriented ("Get My Free Quote" not "Submit")
- **Form labels:** Conversational where appropriate ("Your email" not "Email Address")
- **Error messages:** Helpful, not blaming ("That email doesn't look right — try again?")
- **Empty states:** Encouraging, with clear next action
- **Loading states:** Personality-appropriate ("Crunching the numbers..." for a quote tool)
- **Success states:** Celebrate the win ("Quote sent! Your client will get it in seconds")

## Phase 5: Copy Review Checklist

Before delivering, run every piece of copy through this checklist:

### The Halbert Test
- [ ] Would a starving crowd care about this? (Is there real demand?)
- [ ] Is the big idea clear within 3 seconds?
- [ ] Does every sentence earn its place?

### The Schwartz Test
- [ ] Does the opening match the reader's awareness level?
- [ ] Does the copy channel existing desire (not create new desire)?
- [ ] Is the mechanism clear and named?

### The Hopkins Test
- [ ] Is every claim specific? (Numbers, timeframes, outcomes)
- [ ] Could a competitor say this exact thing? If yes, sharpen it
- [ ] Is there a reason-why for every claim?

### The Sugarman Test
- [ ] Is the first sentence short enough to compel reading the second?
- [ ] Is there a "slippery slide" — does each section pull into the next?
- [ ] Are there unnecessary words that can be cut?

### The Conversion Test
- [ ] Is there ONE clear CTA? (Not three competing actions)
- [ ] Is risk reversed? (Guarantee, free trial, easy cancellation)
- [ ] Is social proof present and specific?
- [ ] Would the reader say "this was written for me"?

## Phase 6: Localisation Layer (NZ Market)

When writing for NZ audiences, apply these adjustments:

- **Tone:** Kiwis distrust hype. Understated confidence beats American-style superlatives. "Pretty bloody good" resonates more than "The #1 Revolutionary Solution"
- **Humour:** Dry, self-deprecating. Never forced. A touch of humour in CTAs or microcopy works well
- **Spelling:** British/NZ English always. Labour, organise, specialise, centre
- **Cultural references:** Use local context — "from the ute", "on-site", "between jobs", "smoko break"
- **Trust signals:** NZ-specific: "NZ-owned", "Built in Aotearoa", "Used by Kiwi tradies from Kaitaia to Invercargill"
- **Anti-patterns:** Avoid: "limited time offer!!!", countdown timers, fake scarcity, aggressive pop-ups. Kiwis see through it and it damages trust
- **Currency:** Always NZD ($). Include GST context where pricing is shown

## Key Principles

- **Clarity over cleverness.** If the reader has to think about what you mean, you've lost them.
- **One idea per piece.** A landing page sells one thing. An ad makes one point. An email has one CTA.
- **Write how they talk.** Read their Reddit posts, their trade forums, their Facebook groups. Steal their exact words.
- **Proof is the new persuasion.** In a sceptical market, one real testimonial outperforms 1,000 words of benefit copy.
- **The offer is the copy.** A great offer with mediocre copy outsells mediocre offer with great copy. Always pressure-test the offer first.
- **Test, don't debate.** Write 3 headlines, run them, let the market decide. Opinions are noise; data is signal.
