# Positioning Angles - Market Positioning Intelligence

This skill guides the discovery of high-conviction market positioning angles using proven direct response and strategic marketing frameworks. The output is a set of distinct, testable positioning angles — each grounded in market reality, emotional resonance, and competitive differentiation.

The user provides context about: what they're building or selling, who the target customer is, and optionally any competitor names or market context they already know.

---

## Phase 1: Context Extraction

Before generating angles, extract as much signal as possible from the user's input. Ask clarifying questions if critical information is missing:

- **What is the offer?** (Product, service, SaaS, agency, info product, physical good)
- **Who is the ideal customer?** (Job title, revenue size, life stage, pain level)
- **What does the customer currently do instead?** (Competitor, workaround, doing nothing)
- **What outcome does the customer desperately want?** (The "after" state)
- **What makes the founder/team uniquely qualified?** (Unfair advantages, lived experience, track record)
- **What's the price point and business model?** (Impacts positioning strategy)
- **Any existing messaging or positioning attempts?** (What's already been tried)

If research data has been provided (Perplexity results, competitor screenshots, customer interviews), absorb all of it before generating angles.

---

## Phase 2: Competitive Landscape Mapping

Before finding your angle, map what's already occupied in the market:

### Saturated Zones (avoid or attack directly)

Identify what the dominant players are saying. Common saturated positions:

- "We're the most affordable"
- "We're the most powerful / feature-rich"
- "We've been around the longest"
- "We work with enterprise"
- "We're the all-in-one solution"

### Whitespace Zones (underserved angles)

Look for positions that are: emotionally resonant but unoccupied, true but unstated, specific to a customer segment being ignored, or built on a unique mechanism no competitor claims.

---

## Phase 3: The 7 Positioning Lenses

Run the offer through each lens. Generate 1-2 specific angle concepts per lens. Not all lenses will be relevant — weight them based on what the market research reveals.

### Lens 1: The Transformation

What does the customer's life look like BEFORE vs AFTER?

- Focus on the emotional and status transformation, not just the functional outcome
- Great positioning lives in the gap between current reality and desired identity
- Format: "From [painful current state] to [aspirational future state]"
- Example: "From chasing work to selecting clients"

### Lens 2: The Enemy

What is the villain in your customer's story — and are you the hero who defeats it?

- The enemy can be a competitor, a category, a belief system, an old way of doing things
- Picking a clear enemy creates tribal belonging and sharpens the offer
- Be specific: "The agency model is broken" lands harder than "we're different"
- Example: "The traditional marketing agency is designed to keep you confused and dependent"

### Lens 3: The Unique Mechanism

What is the specific HOW behind your results — and does it have a name?

- Naming your mechanism creates perceived uniqueness even in commoditised markets
- The mechanism should be: provable, proprietary-feeling, and outcome-linked
- Format: "[Named System/Method/Framework] that produces [specific result]"
- Example: "The 5-Day Build System — a full marketing funnel in one sitting, not one quarter"

### Lens 4: The Niche Claim

Who are you specifically FOR — and can you be the #1 choice for that group?

- Riches are in niches: specificity increases conversion and justifies premium pricing
- A niche can be: industry vertical, company size, geography, founder type, life stage, belief system
- Niching down is not limiting — it's a signal of expertise
- Example: "The only AI marketing agency built for boring businesses doing $2M-$10M"

### Lens 5: The Speed / Simplicity Claim

What takes others months that you do in days? What requires expertise from others that you make simple?

- Speed and simplicity are emotionally powerful because they reduce risk and anxiety
- Must be specific and credible — vague speed claims are ignored
- Example: "Your competitors waited 8 weeks for an agency deck. You'll have a live funnel in 5 days."

### Lens 6: The Social Proof Angle

What proof exists that competitors can't claim?

- Types of proof: specific customer results, contrarian data, founder credentials, volume of customers, notable customers, unusual use cases
- Proof-based positioning is hardest to copy and most trusted by skeptical buyers
- Example: "$100M+ in ad spend managed. Now available to businesses without the $50K retainer."

### Lens 7: The Belief Reframe

What does your customer currently believe that is holding them back — and what truth do you offer instead?

- The best positioning changes the frame, not just the pitch
- Find the false belief your competitors have reinforced, then dismantle it
- Format: "Most [customers] believe [false belief]. Here's what's actually true."
- Example: "Most plumbers think they need to spend $5K/month on ads to grow. The real problem is response time, not reach."

## Phase 4: Angle Scoring Matrix

For each angle generated, score it on these five dimensions (1-5):

| Dimension                  | Question                                                                         |
|----------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Emotional resonance**    | Does this make the customer feel seen and understood?                            |
| **Competitive whitespace** | Is this position unoccupied or underclaimed by competitors?                      |
| **Credibility**            | Can this be proven or demonstrated, not just asserted?                           |
| **Specificity**            | Is this sharp and concrete, or vague and forgettable?                            |
| **Scalability**            | Does this angle work across channels — ads, landing pages, content, sales calls? |

Select the top 2-3 angles with highest combined scores. Flag any angle with a score of 5/5 on emotional resonance + whitespace — these are breakthrough candidates.

---

## Phase 5: Angle Expansion

For the top-scoring angles, expand each into:

**1. One-liner positioning statement**
The core claim in a single sentence. No jargon. No buzzwords.
Format: "[Audience] use [Product] to [Outcome] without [Pain/Tradeoff]"

**2. The 3-sentence pitch**
Sentence 1: Name the enemy or the problem
Sentence 2: Introduce the mechanism or the new belief
Sentence 3: Make the specific promise

**3. Headline variations (3 per angle)**
Write 3 direct-response style headlines using this angle. These are landing page / ad ready.
Use proven headline formulas:

- The Specificity Headline: Exact numbers, timeframes, outcomes
- The Contrast Headline: Old way vs. new way
- The Audience-First Headline: Call out the specific person by name

**4. The objection it pre-empts**
What customer doubt does this angle dissolve before it's even raised?

**5. Where it works best**
Recommend the primary channel for this angle: paid ads, SEO content, cold outreach, referral, partnerships, PR, etc.

---

## Phase 6: Final Recommendation

State the recommended primary positioning angle clearly. Explain:

- Why this angle wins over the alternatives
- What the #1 risk is if they use this angle
- What they should test first (smallest possible experiment)
- What secondary angle to hold in reserve

If the user's business has multiple customer segments, recommend a different angle per segment and flag which one to lead with.

---

## Output Format

Structure output as:

```
## Competitive Landscape Summary
[What's saturated. What's open.]

## The 7 Angles
[Lens name + 1-2 angles each, brief]

## Top 3 Angles (Scored)
[Full scoring matrix]

## Expanded Angles
[Full expansion for top 2-3]

## Recommendation
[Primary angle, rationale, first test]
```

Keep language direct, specific, and marketing-literate. Avoid corporate speak. Write as if you are a senior strategist who has run $100M+ in campaigns and has seen what actually converts — not a generalist trying to sound smart.

---

## Key Principles

- **Specificity beats cleverness.** A dull headline targeting the right person outperforms a witty line targeting no one.
- **The best positions are discovered, not invented.** Listen to what customers already say when they recommend you. Mine that language.
- **One angle wins. Test, don't hedge.** The instinct to use all angles at once kills conversion. Pick one, run it hard, learn, iterate.
- **Emotion opens the door. Logic closes the sale.** Lead with resonance, support with proof.
- **If a competitor could say it, it's not a position.** Keep sharpening until only you can claim it credibly.
